# Portal, Arizona — design basis

Research checked November 2, 2026. Conceptual single-family dwelling assumed in **unincorporated Cochise County** near Portal; parcel jurisdiction is not established. This document guides the study; it is not a permit or a declaration of compliance.

## Adopted codes

County Resolution 26-23 took effect September 1, 2026. Applications submitted on/after that date use the 2024 International Residential Code and related adopted 2024 codes, 2023 NEC, and retained 2012 IECC, with county amendments. Some county pages retain contradictory 2015 text. Use the dated adoption announcement and current ordinance, not the old 2021 design-criteria PDF.

## Site is a controlling design input

Assume a gentle south-falling hillside, freely draining soils, groundwater below foundations and a site outside flood/erosion hazard areas. These assumptions have not been measured. No parcel, slope survey, elevation, utility, easement, zoning district or soil report was supplied. Do not excavate from this study.

County flood guidance requires residential lowest floors, including basements, at/above the regulatory flood elevation and prohibits construction in designated floodway/erosion setback areas. A recessed house may be infeasible on a flood-prone parcel. [County stormwater/floodplain requirements](https://www.cochise.az.gov/869/StormwaterDrainage-Requirements-for-Resi).

Before developing permit drawings: confirm APN/jurisdiction, zoning and setbacks, topo/finished-floor datum, wash setbacks and flood status, soils/expansive or collapsible behavior, groundwater, bearing pressure, lateral soil and hydrostatic loads, slope stability, radon, termite strategy, septic feasibility, well/water rights or service, utility supply and fire-apparatus access. These are unresolved inputs, not assumed approvals.

## Earth-shelter approach

Proposed baseline: **partially underground / earth-bermed**, retaining the north and most of the east/west sides; south front stays exposed. Rear grade is approximately 6 ft above finished floor in the conceptual section; sides taper to open south frontage. Berm toe and slope depend on the soil engineer. Upslope interceptor drainage, exterior waterproofing/protection, footing drain with cleanouts and a verified gravity outlet are separate systems. Roof runoff must not surcharge footing drains.

Baseline uses reinforced-concrete retaining cores with exterior insulation, a capillary break and protected membrane. Tire walls, rammed earth and earthbags are not interchangeable structural substitutions. A soil-covered roof is **not included**: saturation loads, diaphragm, structural design and maintainable waterproofing would need a different roof system.

Technical reference: [CMHA earth-sheltered buildings](https://www.masonryandhardscapes.org/resource/tek-06-07a/) explains earth-shelter drainage and insulation principles; no historical ventilation figures from that page are adopted here. [Sika drainage composite](https://usa.sika.com/sarnafil/en/products-systems/liquid-applied-products/drainage/drainage-composite3811r.html) illustrates the role of a drainage/protection layer, not a specified vendor selection.

Lightweight shed roof, engineered framing running north–south with a bearing line coordinated over the gallery's north edge. Its long living-area opening needs an engineered transfer/header, posts and foundations. Stud and roof-member sizes shown in the model are schematic physical reservations, not verified spans. South overhang is 3 ft; solar shading and wind uplift remain to be calculated.

Balanced mechanical ventilation, ducted heat pump, heat-pump water heater and PV-ready service are planning provisions. No performance or off-grid autonomy claim is made. Cistern/PV/battery/septic/well sizing and graywater approvals require a household/site brief. Do not discharge graywater into the retaining-wall drainage system.

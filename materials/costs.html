Download hours, crews, dependencies and timing

Detailed labor / schedule assumptions
  • Person-hours are the sum of individual workers’ time. A 4-person crew for 40 hours each equals 160 person-hours; crew size is used to derive duration, never multiplied into the labor bill again.
  • Rates are per worker-hour and include an authored allowance for trade employer burden, overhead and normal margin. Travel, minimum callouts and remote-site accommodation may require quote adjustments. Equipment costs are separate; operator labor is in the phase rows.
  • Durations show active crew-work weeks plus separate cure/drying/inspection waits. Schedule depends on preceding phases; parallel crews can overlap. Do not add every phase duration as if all trades were sequential.
  • The hired-crew model assumes each phase crew is available for 40 productive hours per worker per week when scheduled. Its 2–4 week buffer is for ordinary coordination/weather interruptions, not preconstruction design, permit issuance or exceptional supplier delays.
  • Concrete release, temporary bracing and backfill sequence are controlled by the structural/envelope designers. Waiting windows here do not authorize loading concrete after a fixed number of days.
  • Owner-assisted savings assume all marked phase labor is replaced by owner effort. The scenario allows 1.35 times professional person-hours, at 40 combined owner-team hours/week; the resulting extra weeks are calculated along the dependency path. Fabrication/specialist work may still require paid help.
  • Owner-managed cases exclude a GC coordination fee but still require procurement, supervision and inspection coordination. Owner project-management hours are additional to the stated owner hands-on hours.
  • Materials retain supplier-linked benchmarks, quantity/waste rounding and clearly identified provisional allowances. This is a construction estimate; actual material invoices and local labor bids are not yet available.
